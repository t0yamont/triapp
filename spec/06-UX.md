# 06 — UX Specification

## 1. Design principles

1. **The number and its confidence are inseparable.** Every threshold, zone and load
   figure displays its confidence state. An estimated FTP and a tested FTP never look
   identical.
2. **Every change is explained in one sentence, in place.** Not buried in a log. If
   Thursday changed, Thursday says why.
3. **Density over decoration.** The user is an analytical athlete. Show the data.
4. **The dashboard answers three questions:** what am I doing today, am I on track, is
   anything wrong.
5. **Never show a composite score without its components on the same screen.**

**Confidence indicator** — one component, used everywhere a physiological number appears:
a small dot plus label (`Tested · 6 Aug` / `Estimated from training` / `Formula only`).
Tapping it opens a panel explaining what the number is, where it came from, and what
would improve it.

## 3. Navigation

Web: persistent left sidebar.
`Today · Calendar · Activities · Analytics · Races · Settings` and a dimmed `Coach ·
Coming soon` entry (`aria-disabled`, 50% opacity, no pointer events).

## 4. Onboarding

Revised from 11 steps to 8. The original collected fitness numbers before connecting
devices, which asks the athlete for data the app is about to be able to derive.

```
[1] Welcome — what IronFlow does, in three lines. Sign up / log in.

[2] Account — email/password, Google, Apple.

[3] About you — name, DOB, sex, height, mass, units, timezone.
    Medical disclaimer + explicit health-data consent. Both required to proceed.

[4] Connect a device — Garmin (primary), Strava, or upload a history export.
    "Skip for now" permitted but the consequence is stated plainly:
    "Without training history we'll start from formulas, and your first plan will be
     conservative until we've learned more."

[5] Import history — up to 24 months, runs in background with progress.
    The athlete continues; a notification fires on completion.

[6] Availability  ← the highest-value screen in onboarding
    → Which days can you train, and for how long? (per-day minutes)
    → Which days can you get to a pool?
    → Which day is your long ride? Your long run?
    → Gym access? Sauna access?
    → Weekly hours target and hard ceiling.
    This is a hard input to the planner (03-ALGORITHM.md §8.4). Do not make it skippable;
    a plan built on guessed availability will not be followed.

[7] Your numbers — pre-filled from imported history wherever possible, each with its
    confidence badge and provenance. The athlete confirms or corrects.
    Max HR · resting HR · run threshold · FTP/CP · swim CSS.
    "We estimated these from your history. You can correct any of them, and we'll
     schedule tests to confirm."

[8] First race — optional. Name, date, type, priority, expected conditions.

→ Plan generation. Show the actual stages, not a fake spinner:
  Building athlete model → Laying out phases → Placing key sessions →
  Checking safety limits → Done.

→ Dashboard, with a first-run panel: "Here's what we know, here's what we don't,
  and here's the first test we've scheduled."
```

## 5. Screens

### Today (default)

- **Today's session** — name, sport, duration, structure preview, targets in the
  athlete's preferred modality, and *why this session* in one line.
  If the session was adapted: a banner stating the change and its reason, with an undo
  where the change was athlete-initiated.
- **Readiness** — score with its components broken out (HRV, RHR, sleep, wellness),
  each shown against its own baseline band. Never the score alone.
- **This week** — seven-day strip, load done vs planned, S1/S2/S3 distribution vs target.
- **Attention** — only when something is true: a scheduled test, a confidence gate
  blocking intensity, decoupling trending wrong, a plan change awaiting acknowledgement.

### Calendar

Month and week views. Each day: sport chips, planned vs completed, phase colour band,
recovery weeks visually distinct, races marked by priority.
Drag to move a session — on drop, the engine validates, repairs the week if needed, and
shows what it changed before committing.

### Activity detail

- Header: sport, date, duration, distance, the three load figures side by side with
  their disagreement flagged if it exceeds threshold.
- Map with metric-coloured route.
- **Overlay chart**: any two of HR, pace/GAP, power, cadence, altitude, temperature,
  DFA-a1. Shared crosshair, lap markers, drag-to-zoom, selection statistics.
- Zone distribution: 5-zone bar plus the S1/S2/S3 roll-up used for accounting.
- Decoupling panel for sessions ≥75 min, with the first-half/second-half comparison
  drawn explicitly.
- Laps/splits table.
- If this activity completed a planned workout: compliance comparison, target vs actual
  per step.

### Analytics

- **Performance management chart** — CTL/ATL/TSB, combined and per sport, toggleable.
- **Threshold history** — every anchor over time, with confidence bands and test markers.
  This is the screen that shows the product working.
- **Intensity distribution** — rolling 3-week, session-goal and time-in-zone side by side,
  against phase target. The "moderate drift" warning lives here.
- **Durability** — decoupling over time, durability index trend, long-session
  second-half performance.
- **Mean-max curves** — power/pace duration curves, current vs 90 days ago vs all time,
  with the CP model fit overlaid.

### Races

List and detail. Detail shows: countdown, phase position, taper start date, the
prescribed taper shape, expected conditions, whether a heat block is scheduled, and
pacing guidance derived from durability rather than fresh thresholds.

### Settings

Profile, units, timezone, week start · Availability (editable) · Integrations (connect,
disconnect, sync status, last sync) · Zones and thresholds (view, correct, request a
test) · Notifications · **Data** (export everything, delete everything) · Consent
history · Disclaimer.

## 6. Explanation surfaces

Every plan mutation renders from `plan_mutations.reason_text` in one sentence:

- "Moved Thursday's intervals to Friday — your HRV has been below your normal range for
  two days."
- "Reduced this week's volume 10% — you completed 58% of last week and haven't flagged
  any fatigue, so the plan was probably too heavy."
- "Scheduled a 30-minute test on the 14th — your heart rate at threshold pace has
  dropped 4% over three weeks, and I'd rather measure that than assume it."

A "why?" affordance on every such sentence opens the full input snapshot from
`plan_mutations.engine_inputs`. Athletes who understand the reasoning follow the plan.
